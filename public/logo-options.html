SPL Logo Concepts

10 directions for the Super Premier League, Khandwa. All built on your brand colors (black / #FF6B00 orange / white) and Oswald typography. Each is tested against the dark site header. Pick one (or two to shortlist) and I'll refine it into final files: header SVG, favicon, and a single-color jersey version.

01 The Classic Crest

Traditional league shield with stumps and bails. Feels established and official — the "IPL family" look fans instantly trust. Strongest for jerseys and trophies.

02 The Seam

The cricket ball itself as the hero mark, seam stitching detailed in white. Minimal, modern, instantly cricket. The ball alone works as a favicon or app icon.

03 Tiger of MP

Geometric tiger head — Madhya Pradesh is India's Tiger State, so this roots the league in regional pride. Fierce, mascot-ready, and great for merchandise and social media.

04 Fast Bowler

Italic slanted wordmark with motion streaks and a ball full-stop — pure pace and energy. The most "broadcast graphics" option; looks great animated on screens.

05 Hex Badge

Crossed bats and ball inside a hexagon — the classic club-cricket badge, modernized. Balanced and symmetric; embroiders beautifully on caps and jerseys.

06 The Trophy

Championship cup with the ball as negative space in its bowl. Says "this league crowns a winner" — aspiration is the message. Strong for auction and finals branding.

07 Round Seal

Full circular badge with the league name arced over stumps and ball. The "official seal" look — perfect for stamps, stickers, and profile pictures (it's already round).

08 The Comet

Ball streaking upward like a rising star — a metaphor for grassroots talent getting its shot. Dynamic and optimistic; the trail suggests a six flying over the ropes.

09 App Tile

Bold monogram on a solid orange rounded tile with a subtle seam curve. Built for the digital-first league: favicon, app icon, WhatsApp DP — zero detail lost at small sizes.

10 Wicket Strike

Bowled! Stumps shattered, bails flying, ball still in frame. The most dramatic storytelling mark — captures the thrill of the game moment fans live for.

Round 2 — Contrast Palettes

Same league, new color stories. Each concept pairs a dominant color with a deliberate contrast — navy & gold, teal & orange, maroon & gold, and more. All still tested against the dark site background.

11 Royal Navy Crest

Navy & gold — the most premium sports pairing there is (think championship trophies). Serious, established, and prints beautifully on both dark and light jerseys.

12 Teal Storm

Teal lightning bolt with the orange ball as counterpoint — teal is orange's true modern contrast. Energetic and fresh; nothing else in local cricket looks like this.

13 Tricolor Spirit

Saffron, white and green arcs over a chakra-styled ball — patriotic without being literal. For a community league, national pride + local pride is a powerful mix.

14 Maroon Regal

Deep maroon & antique gold with a crown — regal, rooted, very Indian heritage. Feels like a royal trophy tournament; ideal if you want prestige over flash.

15 Electric Blue

The Fast Bowler wordmark re-dressed in electric blue with cyan streaks and an orange ball full-stop. Cool, digital, night-match floodlight energy.

16 The Pitch

A bird's-eye view of the ground — green outfield, tan pitch, stumps at both ends, gold boundary rope. The most literal "we play real cricket here" mark.

17 Sunset Shield

Kite-shaped shield in an orange→magenta→violet gradient — keeps your orange but takes it somewhere modern. The gradient generation's IPL-team aesthetic.

18 Crown & Gold

Gold crown over violet SPL — royalty language for "Premier". Purple + gold is rare in cricket branding, so it stands out instantly on posters and reels.

19 Red Siren

Aggressive red-on-black hex with a swinging bat. Red reads danger and dominance — the "we came to win" option. Strongest for team-rivalry hype content.

20 Sky Chakra

Sky-blue ball radiating chakra rays — sunrise over the ground, new beginnings. Calm, clean, and the friendliest option for a community-first league.
